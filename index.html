Make room

Wave your cursor over the paragraph (or tap it) and the words part around you. You can also drag the stickers in. Every line is measured again and finds somewhere new to stop.

Most web pages decide where a line of text ends before anything moves, and then everything stays put. This paragraph is different. It has no idea where the stickers are. Each time you drag one, every line is measured again, finds the space that is still free, and stops just before it bumps into something. When a sticker lands in the middle of a line, the words split around it and carry on the other side, like water around a stone. None of this asks the browser how big anything is. A small library called Pretext, made by Cheng Lou, works out where every line breaks using plain arithmetic, so the whole page can reflow dozens of times a second without stuttering. Try parking two stickers close together and watch the words squeeze through the gap. Try covering the middle of the paragraph and see how it copes. The text itself is real, and it is just being placed one line at a time, which is why it can bend. If you tilt your head and squint, this is a very small magazine layout that you can rearrange with your fingers.
